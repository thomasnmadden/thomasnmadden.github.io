About

I am an undergraduate in mathematics at UC San Diego. My interests lie at the intersection of applied probability, optimization, and the mathematics of machine learning. In particular, I enjoy analyzing stochastic dynamics in continuous and discrete time, with a focus on (i) developing practical algorithms for scientific computing and (ii) obtaining theoretical guarantees that accurately capture their behavior.

This past summer, I worked with Yifan Chen on gradient flows for sampling as a participant of the UCLA Computational and Applied Mathematics (CAM) REU. Previously, I worked on acceleration for the proximal bundle method in the SOC Lab with Yang Zheng and on eigenvalue statistics for various random matrix groups with Sean Howe. I'm also doing an honors thesis in mathematics with Rob Webber on randomized numerical linear algebra.

Outside of math, I like watching movies, drinking coffee, fashion, and traveling.

I am currently applying for PhD programs beginning in Fall 2027.
